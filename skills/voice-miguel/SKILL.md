# Voice: Miguel

Apply **only** to the artifact Miguel asked for (message, summary, draft, blog
post). Do not restyle the rest of the conversation or unrelated outputs in the
same task.

Miguel writes to customers, partners, colleagues, and a public blog. Warm but
blunt; structured when useful; bilingual EN/ES. Pick the **register** from the
ask (or the channel), then follow that section. Shared rules below always apply.

## Shared (all registers)

1. **Lead with the point.** No throat-clearing.
2. **Match the language of the audience.** Spanish with Spanish speakers
   (casual: "Hola X! Qué bueno leerte."); English otherwise. Don't mix
   formality levels awkwardly.
3. **Plain-spoken.** Admit delays/mistakes in one direct sentence, then move
   on — no groveling.
4. **Warm, never stiff.** First names. Light emoji only where natural
   (🚀 😂 🏎️ 😉) — one max, usually zero in serious threads.
5. **Markdown** for drafts that will paste into Gmail, Slack, Notion, or Docs.
6. **Links:** `[meaningful label](https://...)`. Never raw URLs or "click here".
7. **Self-check:** Would Miguel send this from his phone between meetings (email /
   Slack) or publish it as himself (blog)? If it reads corporate or generic,
   loosen it.

### Anti-patterns (all)

- "I hope this finds you well" / warm regards / corporate boilerplate
- Hedging filler ("just wanted to quickly…", "no worries if not")
- Passive voice for decisions he owns
- Over-apologizing
- Applying **email** rules to a blog post (or vice versa)

## Register: email

**When:** email, customer follow-ups, vendor chasing, formal-ish threads.

1. **Brevity.** Short sentences. One line if one line answers it.
2. **Always close with an action** — next step, question, owner, or deadline.
3. **Multi-topic → numbered list**, one line each.
4. **Openers:** "Hey [Name]," / "Hi [Name]," (even execs). Spanish: "Hola [Name]!"
5. **Body:** 2–4 short paragraphs max, or a numbered list. Lead with the ask.
6. **Questions as a dash list** when gathering input.
7. **Micro-replies OK** (bare link, two-word confirm).
8. **Closers:** "Best, Miguel" or nothing. No "warm regards".
9. **Spelling:** fix obvious typos in email (phone-feel ≠ careless).
10. **Profanity:** none in customer-facing email.

**Tone table**

| Context | Register |
|---|---|
| Customer | Warm-casual, structured, action-closing |
| Escalation | Calm, factual, numbered findings, owner + next step |
| Vendor / chase | Short, increasingly firm, never insulting |
| Thanks / celebration | Genuinely enthusiastic, one emoji max, personal |

**ES opener sample:** "Hola Ana! Qué bueno leerte. Te dejo 3 puntos rápidos:"

## Register: Slack / messaging

**When:** Slack, WhatsApp-style DMs, internal chat, quick handovers.

1. **Burst style.** Several short messages > one composed block. One idea each.
2. **DMs:** lowercase OK, contractions, casual ("NP", "Ok, entonces no."). Don't
   invent typos; don't over-polish.
3. **Status / handover:** still number — `1. CX: … 2. Issue: … 3. Handover: @owner`
4. **Direct pushback is on-brand.** Disagree briefly; give the alternative in
   the same breath.
5. **Coach, don't lecture.**
6. **Customer-facing channels:** same rules but cleaner — full sentences, no
   profanity, still short.
7. **Internal:** mild blunt language OK; keep it professional with customers.

## Register: blog / essay

**When:** Notion blog, essays, opinion pieces, long-form drafts. **Do not** force
action-closing or owner/deadline endings.

Grounded in Miguel's published posts (productivity tips, tech opinion, short
reflective ES pieces).

1. **Punchy open.** First line states the thesis or hook ("Turn notifications
   off.", "Doomscrolling is over.", "This is the way to get shit done…").
2. **Argument, not memo.** Short paragraphs; headings when the piece grows;
   numbered lists for claims or steps — not for "next owners".
3. **Blunt and concrete.** Everyday examples (LinkedIn loop, JetBrains on Win10,
   DND button). Prefer lived detail over abstract management-speak.
4. **Opinion with spine.** Predictions and takes are fine; mark them as such.
   Footnotes / asides when useful.
5. **Casual lexis OK** in personal blog ("shit done", "banger") — tone down for
   guest posts or work-branded long-form if he says so.
6. **Length follows the idea.** A reflective ES note can be three paragraphs;
   a tech take can run long with sections. Don't pad; don't artificially shorten
   a real argument.
7. **Bilingual:** write the whole piece in the locale he asked for (EN or ES);
   don't sprinkle unmotivated code-switching.
8. **Closers:** end on the thought, prediction, or practical tip — not "Best,
   Miguel" and not a corporate CTA unless he asked for one.

**Shapes that fit him**

- How-to / loop-break (problem → why it fails → concrete fix)
- Hot take / prediction (claim → counterarguments → why you're still right)
- Short reflection (one image or analogy, leave it)

## Register: short summary

**When:** TL;DR, handover blurb, meeting note for him to send.

- Default to **email** brevity + numbered structure.
- End with action/owner unless he asked for a neutral digest.
- Match audience language.

---

## Choosing the register

| Cue | Use |
|---|---|
| email, Gmail, reply to customer | email |
| Slack, DM, "ping", handover | Slack / messaging |
| blog, Notion post, essay, "publish" | blog / essay |
| "summary", "TL;DR", "recap" | short summary |
| Unclear | ask one short question, or default to email for outbound messages |

## Out of scope

- Rewriting other people's prose into Miguel's voice unasked
- Applying this skill as a global default for all drafts
- Inventing biographical facts or opinions he didn't imply
